# Multi-Key Sovereignty

## Overview

Constitutional sovereignty may be distributed across multiple keys.
No single key holds absolute authority.

---

## Authority Distribution

### Genesis Authority
Single key class. Permanent. Cannot be distributed.

### Quorum Authority
Multiple keys. Threshold signing.
Distribution prevents single-point compromise.

### Amendment Authority
Derived from quorum. Per-amendment authorization.

### Federation Authority
Per-relationship keys. Bilateral or multilateral.

---

## Threshold Signing

Quorum operations require threshold signatures.
Threshold is defined in the constitutional parameters.

Examples:
- Amendment activation: 2/3 of quorum
- Authority rotation: 3/4 of quorum
- Emergency freeze: unanimous quorum

---

## Sovereignty Invariants

- Genesis authority is singular and permanent
- Quorum authority is distributed and rotatable
- No single quorum key can authorize amendments
- Federation keys are per-relationship

---

## Compromise Response

If quorum keys are compromised:
1. Freeze all amendment operations
2. Rotate quorum authority
3. Re-sign all affected artifacts
4. Record compromise in constitutional registry
