# Federation Protocol v1

## Overview

The federation protocol defines how sovereign constitutional civilizations interoperate without sacrificing constitutional integrity.

Federation does not imply equivalence. Every civilization remains sovereign. Federation only establishes formally verified interoperability boundaries.

---

## Federation Principles

### Sovereignty Preservation
No civilization may alter another civilization's constitutional state. All interaction occurs through explicitly negotiated constitutional treaties.

### Constitutional Verification
Federation requires:
- lineage verification
- specification verification
- replay verification
- treaty compatibility verification

### Semantic Isolation
Different constitutional civilizations may interpret state differently. Federation must never assume semantic equivalence.

### Replay Safety
Federated interactions must remain replayable independently by both civilizations.

---

## Federation Lifecycle

1. Civilization discovery
2. Identity exchange
3. Specification hash verification
4. Lineage verification
5. Treaty negotiation
6. Compatibility determination
7. Boundary establishment
8. Federation certificate issuance

---

## Federation States

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unknown | Civilization not verified |
| Identified | Identity verified |
| Compatible | Treaty-compatible |
| Federated | Active federation established |
| Suspended | Federation temporarily disabled |
| Revoked | Federation permanently revoked |

---

## Federation Invariants

- federation never overrides sovereignty
- replayability must remain intact
- all federation operations are deterministic
- treaties are cryptographically signed
- lineage continuity must remain provable
